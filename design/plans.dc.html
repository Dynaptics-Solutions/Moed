Free and paid · revised

Free is the planner. Paid is your body.

Hours and money stay free — that is the planner people came for, and it works forever without paying. Food, calories, workouts and the watch are a second product living inside the first, and that is the subscription. Seven days of it, free, so nobody has to take it on trust.

Rule one · unchanged
If it costs us money every month, it is paid.

Servers, AI calls, map lookups, stored history. This decided sync, voice, estimates and routes.

Rule two · new
A second product is paid, even when it is cheap to run.

Diet is a whole other app's worth of work. Some of it costs nothing to serve — that is now beside the point.

The fourteen days

Moving diet and activity behind the wall creates one problem: the best thing about them — a day that shortens itself because you slept badly — is impossible to explain in a screenshot. The trial exists to solve exactly that, so it should be spent on it.

Day five is the whole pitch, and it only lands if the trial covers a week with a bad night in it. Seven days is the right length for that reason and no other.

1
No card up front

Asking for payment details to start a free fortnight is how a trial becomes a trap, and this category is already resented for it. Take the card on day seven, from someone who has decided.

2
One reminder, on day thirteen

Saying "your trial ends tomorrow" once is honest. Saying it on days three, five, six and seven is harassment. It also breaks the notification rule the rest of the app follows.

3
It ends by going quiet, not by locking

On day fifteen the diet screens go read-only. Your weight history, your logged food and your meal plan are all still there to look at and export — they just stop taking new entries.

4
The planner never enters the trial

Nothing free should feel borrowed. The trial adds screens; it never touches the day view, so day fifteen cannot feel like something was taken away.

Never behind the paywall

Your own data and getting it out — including every gram and calorie you logged during the trial. Reading everything you have already written. The whole planning loop. If a subscription lapses, the app keeps working on that device: you lose sync, the AI and new food entries, not your history and not your plan.

Worth watching

This split makes the subscription carry more weight, which is good for revenue and bad for word of mouth — the two features people would have told their friends about are now inside the wall. If conversion is fine but growth is slow, the first thing I would test is putting sleep-shortens-your-day back on the free side as the hook.

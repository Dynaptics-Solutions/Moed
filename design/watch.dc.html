On the wrist
Moed on a watch
The whole product answers one question — how full is the day — and that is a glance, not a session. So the watch gets the arc, the next thing, the timer and the evening close. Nothing else.
Can it connect · the honest answer
Wear OS · Android watches
Yes, straightforwardly. A native app, plus a Tile and a complication for the arc. Reads steps and workouts through Health Connect. Samsung, Pixel, Fossil, OnePlus.
One build
watchOS · Apple Watch
Yes. Native app, complications, and a Live Activity for the running timer — which is the single best fit for the wrist. Reads workouts through HealthKit.
One build
Huawei · the awkward one
Also yes, but it is not Wear OS. Huawei watches run HarmonyOS, so it is a separate app in a separate language, built in DevEco Studio and shipped through AppGallery — not Google Play.
A third build
What that means in practice. Wear OS and watchOS are a fortnight each and share the phone's logic. Huawei is a third codebase for one hardware brand — worth it if your users are on Huawei, not worth it on principle. HarmonyOS wearable apps are a documented, supported route — Huawei publishes a wearable getting-started track and watch debugging runs over IP through DevEco Studio and AppGallery Connect — so the question is cost, not possibility.

What the watch deliberately cannot do
Create a record with a length, edit anything, plan a week, or open the meal plan. Capture on a watch means dictating a line that lands in the tray for the phone to sort. Everything here is under three taps, and the arc is readable at arm's length — which is the only test that matters on a wrist.
